# Market, competition, pricing, devices and payments: secondary-school math and exam prep in Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Oman (as of November 2026)

Conventions in these notes. "Paid-report estimate" means a figure from a commercial market-research firm (IMARC, GlobalData, P&S, Mordor, Ken Research). These firms define "edtech" differently and their numbers are not comparable with each other. Currency conversions marked "approx." are my own at the pegs SAR 3.75 = USD 1, OMR 1 ≈ USD 2.60, KWD 1 ≈ USD 3.25. Several news pages show a crawl date (2026) in their header that is not the publication date. Where I could not confirm the publication date, I say so.

## 1. Market size: K-12 edtech and private tutoring spend (Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Oman, GCC)

### Takeaway
No credible source sizes the "secondary math tutoring/edtech" segment in any of the three countries. Paid reports put Saudi edtech anywhere from about USD 0.8bn to 2.6bn (2025), and Saudi online tutoring at about USD 0.1bn (2025). The offline private-tutoring spend that an app would replace is much larger. Kuwait is the most tutoring-intensive market per student: a widely repeated, poorly sourced estimate puts it at KWD 450m a year. Oman has no edtech or tutoring size at all, only private-school figures.

### Inferences
- The headline "edtech" numbers (USD 0.8-2.7bn for Saudi Arabia) mostly cover hardware, LMS and higher-education/corporate e-learning. They do not measure the opportunity for a B2C secondary-math app. Online tutoring (about USD 100m, Saudi Arabia, 2025, paid estimate) is the closest proxy, and even that includes all subjects and grades.
- Per student, Kuwait looks like the richest tutoring market. KWD 450m (if true) spread over roughly 400-500k public-school students is on the order of KWD 1,000 per student per year. That number is unverified and should be quoted as "reported estimate" only. Kuwait is also the smallest market by headcount.
- Saudi Arabia's official 2018 figure (SAR 752m for at-home private lessons) is far below the unofficial SAR 5bn. The truth for 2026 is unknown. The GASTAT number probably undercounts informal cash tutoring and test-prep centres.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for Kuwait's KWD 450m figure (study, year, method).
- No up-to-date (2023+) Saudi official private-lessons spend. HICES 2023 may contain it but was not found in the retrieved tables.
- No Oman tutoring or edtech size from any source.
- No paid-report figure was found specifically for "K-12 test prep" (Qudurat/Tahsili) in Saudi Arabia.

## 2. Competitors for secondary math and exam prep (status as of 2026)

### Takeaway
The field is crowded with live-class and recorded-video platforms. Noon Academy and Abwaab are Saudi-facing, and Baims is Kuwait/Saudi with a university tilt. Free government platforms (Madrasati, Oman's Noor) are everywhere, and global AI photo-solvers are widely used. In 2026 the regional players are consolidating through acquisitions rather than failing: Noon bought Almakhfi, and Abwaab bought Apex and Eduact. I found no Gulf competitor that offers an interactive, act-on-every-screen, step-by-step math tutor in dialect. KAUST is building a Socratic AI math tutor with the Saudi Ministry of Education, and it is the closest conceptual threat.

### Inferences
- The incumbents sell **teachers' time** (live classes, recorded courses, test-prep camps), mostly for Qudurat/Tahsili in Saudi Arabia and university courses in Kuwait. None of the listings I saw offer an interactive step-by-step solver with a hint ladder in dialect. Noon's store listing mentions no AI at all, and Baims' AI only summarises videos.
- Consolidation (Noon + Almakhfi, Baims + Orcas, Abwaab + Apex/Eduact) suggests the regional players think scale and test-prep content win. It also makes them potential acquirers or partners for a differentiated tutor engine.

- Kuwait and Oman have no strong local secondary-math app. Abwaab does not list either curriculum. That is a gap, but it may reflect small headcount rather than neglect.

## 3. Gaps: what existing products do badly for math

### Takeaway
There is little hard evidence (no review mining or press study) on math-specific weaknesses of Gulf products. Three signals exist. Users complain about price and about long recorded lessons. LLMs are measurably weaker at Arabic math. And there is strong experimental evidence that answer-giving AI harms learning while guarded, hint-based AI does not. That last point supports the hint-ladder design directly.

### Cited Findings
- Noon Academy Saudi reviews (visible reviews are 2020-2023): users ask for lower course prices and complain that recorded courses are too long. They praise the multiple-choice practice and the choice of teachers. — [App Store SA](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/id1214874641?l=ar-eg)
- Bastani et al., PNAS 2025 (vol. 122, e2422633122): field experiment with nearly 1,000 high-school math students in Turkey (Fall 2023). Students given an unguarded GPT-4 "GPT Base" tutor scored 17% lower once access was removed. A "GPT Tutor" with learning safeguards (hints, no direct answers) "largely mitigated" the harm. A secondary account says GPT Base users solved 48% more practice problems. — [Penn CHIBE](https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/generative-ai-without-guardrails-can-harm-learning-evidence-from-high-school-mathematics); [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)
- Nile University (2024) study of Arabic math problem solving: LLMs "still require enhancements to achieve satisfactory performance" in Arabic math. Synthetic Arabic data helps, and annotated Arabic datasets are scarce. — [Nile University](https://www.nu.edu.eg/publications/mathematical-problem-solving-arabic-assessing-large-language-models)
- GAGA explicitly positions against "non-interactive online education platforms" and traditional private tutoring. This is a competitor's framing of the same gap. — [TechMoran](https://techmoran.com/2026/04/10/saudi-edtech-startup-gaga-raises-2-5-million-to-scale-live-learning-platform/)
- KAUST's tutor design ("asks questions that lead the learner toward the correct answer") shows the Saudi establishment sees the same gap. — [KAUST](https://www.kaust.edu.sa/news/return-of-socrates-kaust-developing-ai-education-for-personalized-learning)
- Kuwaiti students cite hard exams and weak teaching as the main reasons for private tutoring (field study of 1,634 students and 257 public-school teachers, published in Al-Majalla Al-Tarbawiya, 2013). Attribution comes from a search summary; the record was not opened. — [e-Marefa record](https://search.emarefa.net/detail/BIM-644075)
- A Sabq investigation headline about Saudi Qudurat/Tahsili courses ("large sums of money and unknown trainers... courses under the microscope") points to quality and price concerns in test-prep courses. I could not open the article (HTTP 403). — [Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/BemSASw)

### Inferences
- The defensible gap is "understanding for struggling students, at low cost, without a live teacher". Incumbents are live or recorded teacher-led (expensive, long, passive). Global AI solvers give answers (the Bastani result suggests this harms learning) and are weaker in Arabic. Nobody explains in Gulf dialect.
- Dialect is a real but untested differentiator. Saudi (Najdi/Hijazi), Kuwaiti and Omani dialects all differ from Tunisian derja, so the content and tutor persona would need re-localisation per country. Exam papers in all three countries are in Arabic, not French (to be confirmed by the curriculum researchers).

### Gaps
- No systematic app-store review analysis of math products in Arabic was found. A 1-2 day manual review scrape of Noon, Abwaab, Baims, Photomath and Gauth in the SA/KW/OM stores would close this.
- No press criticism specific to math learning quality on these platforms was found.
