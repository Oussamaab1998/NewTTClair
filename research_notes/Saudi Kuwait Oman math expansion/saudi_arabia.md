# Saudi Arabia: secondary mathematics, high-stakes exams and the exam-prep market (state as of November 2026)

Scope: grades 10-12 of Saudi public general education (نظام المسارات), the national admission tests run by ETEC/Qiyas (Qudurat, Tahsili), student counts, math notation as it affects a KaTeX LTR renderer, and study/tutoring habits. Research date: 2026-11-17. Many official pages (etec.gov.sa, moe.gov.sa study plans) refused automated fetches or returned 404, so several facts rely on press, ETEC statements quoted by the press, or commercial prep sites. Each item says which.

## 1. Structure of secondary math (grades 10-12, Secondary Tracks system, textbooks, calendar)

### Takeaway
Saudi secondary school is 3 years (grades 10-12). Under the Tracks system (نظام المسارات), which started with grade 10 in 2021/22, grade 10 is a common year and grades 11-12 are split into 5 tracks: General, Computer Science & Engineering, Health & Life, Business Administration, Sharia. The three science-type tracks share one strong math sequence that ends in grade 12 with limits, derivatives, integrals, vectors, polar/complex numbers, probability and statistics. The Business and Sharia tracks carry little or no advanced math (Statistics in Business). Textbooks are Arabic adaptations of the McGraw-Hill series. Public schools went back from three terms to **two semesters** from 2025/26, and 2026/27 also has two semesters, so the math books are now split into two volumes per year (for example Math 3-1 and 3-2 in grade 12).

### Cited Findings
**System and tracks**
- Saudi education is organized 6-3-3. Secondary covers grades 10-12, and compulsory schooling runs from age 6 to 15. The TIMSS 2023 encyclopedia names "multiple learning pathways ... at the upper secondary stage" as a major reform. — [TIMSS 2023 Encyclopedia: Saudi Arabia](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Saudi-Arabia.pdf)
- The MoE study-plans guide (Curriculum Development Center, 3rd edition, file name "Study-plans-1445") describes a common first year followed by 2 years in 5 main tracks: General (العام), Sharia (الشرعي), Business Administration (إدارة الأعمال), Computer Science & Engineering (علوم الحاسب والهندسة), Health & Life (الصحة والحياة). Only the search snippet was seen: the PDF returned 404 when fetched. — [MoE study plans guide PDF](https://www.moe.gov.sa/ar/education/generaleducation/StudyPlans/Documents/Study-plans-1445.pdf)
- Timing: a July 2022 explainer says the system "started last year in first secondary" (that is 2021/22), that grade 10 is common to all, and that students are placed into one of the 5 tracks in grade 11 based on an interest inventory (مقياس الميول). — [X thread, Emad Al-Sharif, Jul 2022](https://x.com/3madAlshareef/status/1549362239693545472)

### Inferences
- **Content target.** For the General, CS & Engineering and Health & Life tracks, grade-12 math is a US-style precalculus plus intro calculus (McGraw-Hill Precalculus). It is broader but shallower than the Tunisian Bac Math section:
  - Limits and derivatives appear only in the last chapter.
  - Integration stops at area under a curve and the FTC.
  - Vectors, polar/complex numbers, conics, probability distributions (binomial, normal) and statistics are all in scope.
- **Exam content.** Tahsili draws on grades 10-12, so the useful "exam" content is the whole 3-year sequence, not one final year.
- **Textbook churn.** The books were re-split from three to two volumes per year in 1447. Lesson numbering (e.g., 3-2 ch 8) is current for 2025/26-2026/27 but changed recently. Any content keyed to chapter/lesson IDs must use the 1447+ editions.
- **Which students to target.** Business and Sharia students (no advanced math) are a weak target. The math-relevant population is the 3 science-type tracks plus everyone for the Qudurat quantitative section.

## 2. Exams: no national exit exam; Qudurat and Tahsili (ETEC/Qiyas), admission weighting, Nafes

### Takeaway
I found no national end-of-secondary exam. The high-stakes gates are two national admission tests run by ETEC's National Center for Assessment (Qiyas):
- **Qudurat** (General Aptitude Test): 120 multiple-choice questions, about 2 h. The quantitative part is about 45% of the science version and is arithmetic, geometry, algebra and data. Paper or digital, 5 attempts, valid 5 years.
- **Tahsili** (Achievement Test): about 110 multiple-choice questions on grades 10-12 Math, Physics, Chemistry and Biology, about 2 h 05. Math is roughly 20-27% of the test (reports conflict).

University composite scores typically weight school GPA 30%, Qudurat 30%, Tahsili 40%, but this varies a lot by university. Official past papers are not published; the market runs on unofficial "tajmee'at" (recalled-question collections). Nafes (national assessments) covers grades 3, 6 and 9 only.

### Cited Findings
**Qudurat (General Aptitude Test)**
- Format (Mualimi guide, Aug 2026, citing ETEC/Qiyas without links):
  - 120 MCQs in 5 sections of 24, 2 h 05 min.
  - Arabic or English version.
  - Science and literary versions.
- Quantitative share by version:
  - Science version: arithmetic 23%, geometry 10%, algebra 4%, data interpretation 8% (about 45% quantitative).
  - Literary version: arithmetic 13%, geometry 8%, data 4% (about 25%).
- Paper vs digital:
  - Paper has fixed windows and results in about a month.
  - Digital is offered all year with results in 3-5 working days.
- Fees: SAR 100 paper early, 150 paper late, 150 digital, 200 abroad.
- Eligibility: grades 11-12 and graduates.
- Commonly cited composite: GPA 30% / Qudurat 30% / Tahsili 40%, varying by university.
- Source for all of the above: [Mualimi: Qudurat guide 2026](https://www.mualimi.app/en/blog/qudurat-gat-exam-complete-guide)
- Attempts (ETEC on X, quoted by Ajel): 5 attempts for students from grade 11 up, with at most 4 on either the paper or the digital version. — [Ajel](https://ajel.sa/thehour/5pij0lduze)
- A third-party practice site gives a different structure: about 120 questions on paper vs 96 on computer, about 52 quantitative. Low reliability. — [open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/practice/sa-qiyas)
- An English Qudurat exists. Students from British/American/IB schools must also sit Qudurat and Tahsili for public universities. — [PapaMarks, Aug 2026](https://papamarks.com/blog/qudurat-tahsili-british-curriculum-saudi)

### Inferences
- **Effective "bac" for a Saudi student = school GPA + Qudurat + Tahsili.**
  - Math matters in two forms: (a) Qudurat quantitative, which is mental arithmetic, ratios, geometry and data reading, speed-focused, no calculator assumed, about 45% of a test that every university applicant takes; (b) the Tahsili math section, which is curriculum MCQs across grades 10-12.
  - Neither format is the Tunisian long-form written proof style. Content would need MCQ-first formats with timed practice, plus step-by-step understanding behind each MCQ.
- **Addressable test-taker volume (estimates):**
  - Tahsili: 300k+ per year. 334k in 2020; the grade-12 cohort is now about 490k, and only the science-type tracks need the scientific Tahsili.
  - Qudurat: higher, since nearly every applicant takes it, often several times.
  - These are estimates; recent official counts were not found.
- **No real past papers.** Since ETEC does not publish real past papers, prep sites sell "تجميعات" (student-recalled question banks; e.g., "bank of 120 models"). A product would need original items that mirror ETEC's published blueprint, not copied items.

## 3. Numbers: students, grade-12 cohort, schools, sector split, TIMSS/PISA

### Takeaway
- General education has about 6.4-6.7M students. Secondary (grades 10-12) has about 1.46M students: 746k boys and 712k girls (MoE data via Argaam, Oct 2024).
- The 2026 graduating class was 493,862, of whom 84.5% were Saudi and about 8 in 10 were in government schools.
- Private enrollment is roughly 17-28% depending on source. The MoE counts 36,800 schools in total; I found no count of secondary schools.
- Math achievement is low. TIMSS 2023: grade 4 = 420 (+22 vs 2019), grade 8 = 397. PISA 2022: 389 (+16 vs 2018), with only 30% at Level 2 or above. PISA 2025 math was flat vs 2022 (32% at Level 2 or above).

## 4. Language and notation (renderer-critical)

### Takeaway
Saudi secondary math, which descends from McGraw-Hill, is written in Arabic prose (RTL) with **Latin-letter variables and function names (x, y, f(x), θ, sin, cos, tan) and Western digits (0-9)**. Formulas read left-to-right, and exercise sub-parts often carry Latin letters "(a)". Unlike the app's Tunisian rule ("math always its own block"), Saudi materials routinely put short LTR math *inline* inside Arabic sentences. Older Arabized notation (س، ص) is legacy, not current. Qudurat is offered in Arabic or English. Some private schools teach the "Saudi stream" math in English.

### Cited Findings
- **Saudi teachers' worksheet pack for Math 3-2** (grade 12, Tracks; trig identities, conics, vectors; includes "تحصيلي رياضيات" practice). Text extraction of its 118 pages shows:
  - Latin function names and Greek letters: `tan θ = sin θ / cos θ`, `cos²θ + sin²θ = 1`, `sin(π/2 − θ) = cos θ`.
  - MCQ options written as `f(x) = cos x`, `f(x) = sin x`.
  - A textbook-style word problem in Arabic with inline Latin variables: "إذا كان عدد ساعات النهار ... هو d ، ويمكن تمثيلها بالمعادلة d = 3 sin(2π/365 t) + 12 ... (a".
  - Zero Arabic-Indic digits (٠-٩) in about 317k characters of text; all digits are Western.
  - Latin-letter sub-question labels "(a".
  - Source: [mnhaji worksheet PDF](https://mnhaji.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/worksheet-alriadiaat3-2.pdf), analysed by me.
  - Caveat: a teacher-made worksheet that copies the textbook's notation, not the textbook PDF itself.
- Textbook-mirror sites print lesson numbers in Western digits ("5-1", "8-6"). Headings sometimes use Arabic-Indic digits (site typography, not textbook content). — [mnhaji Math 3-2](https://www.mnhaji.com/%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%AB%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AB-%D8%AB%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%8A-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%812/); [salisedu](https://salisedu.com/6374/)
- A Saudi columnist (Jun 2023) recalls Arabized math curricula using "س" and "ص" (sometimes "ع"), and since intermediate school "heavy use of X and Y". This is anecdotal on the shift from Arabic to Latin letters. — [Jehat, Amir Al-Salih](https://jehat.net/?act=artc&id=100869)
- Arabic mathematical notation (س for x, ص for y, Arabic function names, RTL formulas) exists as a convention in parts of the Arab world. — [Arabic Wikipedia: التدوين الرياضي العربي](https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%B2_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6%D9%8A_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%A8%D9%8A)
- The curriculum is McGraw-Hill-derived (translated and harmonised) since the late 2000s. — [ERIC EJ1089809](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1089809.pdf); [TIMSS 2023](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Saudi-Arabia.pdf)
- English is taught from grade 1 (TIMSS 2023). Qudurat has an English version. — [Mualimi](https://www.mualimi.app/en/blog/qudurat-gat-exam-complete-guide); [PapaMarks](https://papamarks.com/blog/qudurat-tahsili-british-curriculum-saudi)
- **English-medium "Saudi stream"** exists in some private schools:
  - Nada School's Grade 11 "Saudi Stream" math syllabus is in English, with McGraw-Hill chapter titles: "Analyzing Functions", "Exponential and Logarithmic Relations and Functions". — [Nada School PDF](https://nadaschool.edu.sa/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Grade-11-Saudi-Stream_compressed.pdf)
  - Al Aziziyah Private Schools teach math and science in English (Pearson materials) alongside the MoE national curriculum. — [mdares.ai](https://mdares.ai/sa-en/schools/al-aziziyah-private-schools)
  - Saad National Schools teach math and science in English on top of the MoE core. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saad_National_Schools)
  - No MoE regulation on this was found.

### Inferences
- **Renderer.** KaTeX LTR with Latin symbols and Western digits is the right default for Saudi Arabia; there is no need for an Arabic-letter (س/ص) math mode. The app's "math in its own LTR block" rule still works for display formulas.
- **Inline math is unavoidable.** Saudi prose constantly embeds short LTR fragments inside RTL sentences (a variable name "d", "f(x)", "(a"). The bidi isolation work (U+2066-U+2069 or separate LTR runs) is unavoidable for Saudi content; the Tunisian workaround of never mixing will produce unnatural text.
- **Tahsili notation.** Tahsili/Qudurat Arabic items very likely use the same notation as the textbooks, since prep "tahsili" items in the worksheet do. This is not verified on an official ETEC sample.
- **Terminology.** Math terms are in Arabic (النهايات، الاشتقاق، المتجهات، التوزيع الطبيعي), not French or English. Function names (sin, cos, tan, log, lim presumably) stay Latin. A Saudi "explanation language" would be Gulf/Najdi Arabic or MSA, a new localisation layer, not derja. French terms disappear entirely.

### Gaps
- Did not open an official textbook PDF (ien.edu.sa / Madrasati) to confirm:
  - "lim" vs "نها" for limits,
  - "dx" for integrals,
  - decimal separator (point vs Arabic comma),
  - interval notation.
- Did not see an official ETEC Tahsili/Qudurat sample item to confirm notation.
- MoE policy on English-medium math in private schools not found.

## 6. Reforms 2025-2027 affecting secondary math or admission exams

### Takeaway
- **Calendar.** Public schools returned to two semesters from 2025/26 (Council of Ministers, 5 Aug 2025), framed over a four-year calendar, so 2026/27 is two semesters as well. Textbooks were re-split into two volumes per year (1447 "new editions").
- **Tracks.** The Tracks system (since 2021/22) is now fully rolled out through grade 12.
- **Exams.** No announced 2026-27 change to the Qudurat/Tahsili model or to admission weighting was found. The main observable change is a reported Tahsili format of about 110 questions, aligned with the Tracks system.

### Cited Findings
- **Two semesters**
  - Council of Ministers approval on 5 Aug 2025 for general-education schools.
  - The four-year calendar frame (1447-1450 AH, to 2029) was kept.
  - Private and international schools are free to choose their own system.
  - Justification: quality depends on teachers, curriculum and governance more than on the number of terms; the 180-day minimum is kept.
  - Sources: [MoE news](https://www.moe.gov.sa/ar/mediacenter/MOEnews/Pages/news1_05082025.aspx); [MoE Riyadh](https://sites.moe.gov.sa/Riyadh/news/news-165/); [Argaam: calendars for 1447 and 1448](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/1833639)
- Al-Riyadh reports the MoE intends to carry over strengths of the 3-term system: activities, skills and continuous short quizzes. — [Al-Riyadh](https://www.alriyadh.com/2145191)
- **New textbook editions:** grade-12 books issued as a "new edition" for semester 1 of 1447 (e.g., Math 3-1). — [mnhaji on X](https://x.com/mnhajicom/status/1957641403346915355)
- **ETEC school evaluation:** ETEC's national school evaluation (Nafes plus school report cards) was praised by an OECD expert team lead as "unprecedented in speed and comprehensiveness" (SPA, 2025). It covers primary and intermediate schools. — [SPA](https://spa.gov.sa/N2327919)
- **Tahsili format:** current reports give about 110 questions in 5 sections, versus older descriptions of 95 questions/180 min (2023) and 8 sections of 5-6 questions (the 2020 remote version). This suggests a recent restructuring, but no official announcement was found. — [Dirasa Abroad](https://www.dirasaabroad.com/achievement-test/); [Mujtahid 2023](https://mujtahidacademy.com/blog/articles/tahsili-preparation-guide.html)

### Inferences
- **Stable through 2026/27.** The exam regime (Qudurat + Tahsili + GPA composite) appears stable for 2026/27, so content built now would not be invalidated by an exam overhaul within a year.
- **Curriculum is the moving part.** Two-semester re-editions and Tracks naming are what changes. Content should map to topics and lesson titles rather than hard-coded book/page numbers.

### Gaps
- Not found: any official 2025-2027 announcement on changing Qudurat/Tahsili structure or weights, introducing a national grade-12 exam, extending Nafes to secondary, or AI-related policy for student tools.
- Not found: regulations on data of minors for edtech apps in Saudi Arabia (the PDPL applies generally; not researched here).
