# Start in Kuwait, earn Saudi later

Kuwait should come first, Saudi Arabia second, and Oman should wait. Kuwait's Grade 12 Science math exam is a centrally set written paper on limits, derivatives, integrals and statistics. It is marked step by step in half points, and stamped Ministry model answers circulate freely. That makes it the closest thing in the Gulf to the Tunisian bac, and a near fit for TTeClair's engine-checked, line-by-line lessons. Kuwait is small, at roughly **20,000–29,000 Science students a year**, but it is rich: one tutoring hour costs **KWD 10–30 (USD 33–98)**, iPhones carry about **39% of mobile web traffic**, there is no VAT, and its data rules are the lightest of the three. Saudi Arabia is about **12 times bigger (493,862 secondary graduates in 2026)** and also pays well. But its high-stakes math sits inside two multiple-choice admission tests, Qudurat and Tahsili. Their real questions are never published and may not legally be reused, and the market is held by funded rivals such as Noon Academy, with a KAUST–Ministry AI math tutor in development. Saudi Arabia therefore needs a different content model and a heavier legal setup. Oman's Advanced math course (**28,659 exam candidates in 2024/25**) overlaps well with Tunisian content. But its textbooks write math right to left, with Arabic letters and Arabic-Indic digits, which the current left-to-right renderer cannot show, and **81% of Omani mobile traffic is Android**. By our own estimate, an iOS-only Kuwait launch at USD 15/month would bring in about **USD 17,000–86,000 a year gross (mid case about USD 43,000)**. So Kuwait is a cheap test of whether TTeClair travels, not a business on its own. Before writing any Gulf content, the next step is three cheap checks: a Kuwaiti lawyer's opinion on using past papers, a Kuwaiti math teacher, and a two-exercise test with real Grade 12 students.

## Kuwait: a small, rich market with a step-marked calculus exam

### Grade 12 Science is a full calculus year split into two exam halves

Kuwaiti secondary school runs Grades 10–12 (ages 15–17) in two semesters. Grade 10 is common to everyone. From Grade 11 students split into Science and Literary, and a small religious stream also exists ([TIMSS 2023 Encyclopedia: Kuwait](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Kuwait.pdf)). Public schools and Arabic private schools follow one national curriculum taught in Arabic. Foreign-curriculum private schools (British, American, Indian) do not sit the national exam. Science students get **6 math periods of 45 minutes a week** in Grades 10–12 and literary students 2 periods in Grades 11–12, over a short **30-week** school year (same source).

The Ministry's own syllabus plan for **Grade 12 Science, semester 1, 2026/27** has four units over **76 periods** ([MOE syllabus plan PDF](https://adinuae.com/download/a49d88ded159f03ae61c0e00f821a589/1788772409.pdf)):

| Unit | Lessons |
|---|---|
| Limits and continuity | Limits; limits at ±∞; indeterminate forms; trig limits |
| Differentiation | Rules; trig derivatives; chain rule; higher-order and implicit derivatives |
| Applications of the derivative | Absolute extrema; increasing and decreasing; linking f′ and f″ to the graph; graphing polynomials; optimisation |
| Statistics | Estimation; hypothesis tests. Correlation and regression are suspended entirely for 2026/27 |

Each lesson in the plan points to exact pages of the student book and workbook, and dropped exercises are marked. That gives a ready-made skill map that also tells you what is out of scope each year. Semester 2 covers integration (indefinite, substitution, by parts, definite), applications of integration, and conic sections ([kwedufiles](https://kwedufiles.com/id=12463)). Grade 11 Science covers real numbers, real functions, exponentials and logs, and vectors, a list seen only in search snippets of the Ministry e-library ([MOE e-library](https://elibrary.moe.edu.kw/api/File/preview/book/427)).

For TTeClair this is the best overlap in the Gulf: limits, derivatives, variations, optimisation and integrals are all Tunisian bac ground. The new pieces are inferential statistics and conics. No Grade 12 probability unit was found, so Tunisian probability exercises such as EX2 do not carry over.

### The paper is marked in half points per step, which is how TTeClair teaches

Grades 10–12 exams are set by the Ministry. Grade 12 papers are marked at national level, with names replaced by ID numbers. The final average counts Grade 10 at 10%, Grade 11 at 20% and **Grade 12 at 70%** ([TIMSS 2023 Kuwait](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Kuwait.pdf)). One aggregator claims 10/10/80 ([kwhashtag](https://kwhashtag.com/13933)), but the TIMSS chapter and the 2026 draft regulation both say 10/20/70.

Each semester mark is reportedly 30 coursework plus 70 exam. A 2026 draft regulation would change that to **40/60**, add a second exam round for up to three failed subjects, and bar families from reviewing papers. Its adoption was not confirmed ([Al-Rai, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1770530/%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AA/%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D9%86%D8%AD%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%BA%D9%8A%D9%8A%D8%B1)).

The stamped official model answer for the **2024/25 semester-1 Science paper** shows the format ([official answer key](https://kwedufiles.com/download/1412c739dd72fc40e802f315061441be/1736068834.pdf)). The paper runs **2 hours 45 minutes** over 11 pages. The essay part opens with a Q1 worth 15 marks on its own: 7 for a limit solved by synthetic division, and 8 for a quotient-rule derivative plus an implicit derivative. Marking gives **1 or ½ mark per step**, and alternative methods earn full credit. The objective part has **10 one-mark items**, three true/false and seven 4-option MCQs, on limits at infinity, differentiability of a piecewise function, extreme values, composition, monotonicity and a confidence interval for μ. This is very close to the product's current shape. The essay part maps onto line-by-line writing checked by the engine, and the objective part maps onto option questions.

Exam security is tight. In June 2026 about **41,599** students sat in 170 committees. Exam boxes travelled from a secret printing press on 220 GPS-tracked buses, and phones and smartwatches were banned ([Al-Jarida, 16 Jun 2026](https://www.aljarida.com/article/134761)). In January 2026, **33,476** students sat semester 1, and Science students started with math on 4 January ([Al-Rai, 3 Jan 2026](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/1752066/%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AA/%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9/33476-%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A7-%D9%88%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D9%8A%D8%A4%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%AA%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AB%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D8%B4%D8%B1-%D8%BA%D8%AF%D8%A7)). Results are published on the Ministry site and the government Sahel app ([Al-Jarida, 18 Jan 2026](https://www.aljarida.com/article/120468)).

That gives Kuwait two exam seasons a year, early January and June, and two selling windows before them.

That clashes with the 53 perfect scores reported a year earlier. The likely explanation is that "3" counts only Kuwaiti or public-school students, but this is not verified.

Either way, the trend is clear: passing is no longer the problem. The pressure is to reach the averages that Medicine (at least 85%) and Engineering (at least 80%) demand.

That changes the Kuwait pitch. In Tunisia, TTeClair is mainly a rescue for students who are struggling. In Kuwait it also has to work as a tool for pushing a good student from 85% to 95%+. TIMSS results (below) show plenty of weak students too, so the product should serve both groups.

### Kuwait University adds a math test worth up to 20% of admission

Kuwait University ranks applicants by an "equivalent average". It combines the school percentage with the university's own aptitude tests in English and Math ([KU admission rules 2026/27](https://portal.ku.edu.kw/manuals/admission/en/ku_colleges_rules.pdf)):

| College | School % | English test | Math test | Extra condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engineering & Petroleum | 65% | 15% | **20%** | School average ≥ 80% |
| Pharmacy, Public Health | 70% | 15% | 15% | School average ≥ 80% |
| Architecture, Life Sciences, Business | 70% | 15% | 15% | |
| Medicine, Dentistry | 75% | 15% | 10% | School average ≥ 85% |

Aptitude scores are valid for two years, the best score counts, and the pass mark in math is 50% ([KU student guide](https://www.ku.edu.kw/sites/default/files/2026-01/st.guide_.en_.pdf); [CEM manual](https://www.ku.edu.kw/sites/default/files/2025-11/CEM%20Student%20Manual%20Eng.pdf)). A third-party prep site says the math test has 20 MCQs in 60 minutes, with no calculator, for about KWD 10 ([open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/practice/kw-ku-math-aptitude)). This is not officially confirmed, and no official syllabus was found.

A KU math-test module is a natural second product for the same students. Its value can even be stated in admission points.

### About 25,000 Science students a year, with weak foundations

The 2026/27 school year opened with **481,511 students** in government, Arabic private and religious schools. Foreign-curriculum schools are not included in that count ([Kuwait Times, 13 Sep 2026](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/49669/kuwait/kuwait-education-ministry-ready-to-welcome-481000-students/)).

The Ministry's live dashboard shows **115,302 general-secondary students**, 78,847 Kuwaiti and 36,455 non-Kuwaiti, so about 32% are non-Kuwaiti but still Arabic-medium. It also counts 1,229 schools, with private Arabic schools holding **17.58%** of students, though its regional tables do not add up to its own headline totals ([MOE dashboard](https://eservices.moe.edu.kw/app/dashboard/dashboard_sis.xhtml)).

The Grade 12 cohort sitting the national exam moves between about 33,500 and 43,900 a year. In 2023 it was 26,576 Science and 17,314 Literary ([Kuwait Times, Jun 2023](https://kuwaittimes.com/grade-12-finals-underway-as-moe-battles-cheating/)). In January 2025 it was 29,180 Science, 13,955 Literary and 770 religious students ([Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/86221)). So Science is **61–66%** of the cohort, or roughly **20,000–29,000 students a year**. The drop to 33,476 in January 2026 is unexplained.

Kuwait sits near the bottom of TIMSS 2023: **382 in Grade 4** against an international average of 503, and **399 in Grade 8** against 478. The Grade 8 score is flagged for reliability concerns ([TIMSS 2023 G8 exhibit](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/1-2-1_ach-g8m-dist.pdf); [G4 exhibit](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/1-1-1_ach-g4m-dist.pdf)). Many students reach Grade 12 with gaps in the basics, which supports TTeClair's "I don't know" detours into prerequisites.

### Math is in Latin letters, but it sits inside Arabic sentences

The official 2024/25 answer key shows notation the current renderer already handles ([official answer key](https://kwedufiles.com/download/1412c739dd72fc40e802f315061441be/1736068834.pdf)). Display math is fully Latin and left to right: `lim` with `x→2` underneath, `g′(x) = (cos x + x sin x)/(cos x)²`, `dy/dx`, `μ`, `∞`, `f∘g`. Digits are Western, the decimal separator is a point, sub-parts and MCQ options are labelled (a)–(d), and instructions are in Modern Standard Arabic. Some administrative headers, such as the year on the syllabus plan, use Arabic-Indic digits (٢٠٢٦/٢٠٢٧). French appears only as a foreign language in the Literary section ([TIMSS 2023 Kuwait](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Kuwait.pdf)).

The catch is that Kuwaiti papers put short math **inside** Arabic sentences. One MCQ, for example, gives f(x) = ax² − 25x and a local extremum at x = 5/2, all in the middle of an Arabic sentence, and asks for a. Intervals such as (0, ∞) and inequalities such as 62.84 < μ < 69.46 also sit mid-sentence. Those are exactly the fragments whose order can flip in right-to-left text.

The project's rule that math is always its own block would force rewriting every item. The project has already measured, on the iOS simulator, that isolated short left-to-right runs inside Arabic display correctly (docs/DECISIONS.md, D3 update of 2026-10-21). Android remains unmeasured.

Language is the other change. Explanations would be in Kuwaiti dialect with Arabic math terms (النهاية، الاشتقاق، القيمة القصوى، الفترة، القسمة التركيبية، صيغة غير معينة، قابلة للاشتقاق، تزايد/تناقص), not derja with French terms. None of the Tunisian explanation text can be reused as is.

### Tutoring is banned for teachers, common anyway, and costs KWD 10–30 an hour

Kuwait has the highest tutoring rate in the Gulf: **55% of Grade 8 students**, against 50% in Saudi Arabia and 40% in Oman. The survey year was not named ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/did-you-know-kuwait-has-the-highest-rate-of-private-tutoring-in-the-gulf)). A volunteer group estimated in 2018 that **over 90% of secondary students** take tutoring in science subjects ([Al Jazeera, 2018](https://www.aljazeera.net/lifestyle/2018/12/23/%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%83%d9%88%d9%8a%d8%aa-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%af%d8%b1%d9%88%d8%b3-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%ae%d8%b5%d9%88%d8%b5%d9%8a%d8%a9-%d9%85%d8%b9%d9%84%d9%85%d9%88%d9%86)). Most tutors are the students' own school teachers ([Al-Rai, 2016](https://www.alraimedia.com/article/693502/%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF/%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B5%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%8A%D8%A9)).

Prices are high. In 2018 a secondary session cost up to KWD 30, one mother paid KWD 80 a week for math and English, and a semester physics package cost about KWD 500 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.net/lifestyle/2018/12/23/%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%83%d9%88%d9%8a%d8%aa-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%af%d8%b1%d9%88%d8%b3-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%ae%d8%b5%d9%88%d8%b5%d9%8a%d8%a9-%d9%85%d8%b9%d9%84%d9%85%d9%88%d9%86)). Current listings show KWD 10–30 an hour ([shrkte](https://www.shrkte.com/private-teacher-kuwait/)) and 16–32 at an agency ([TutorChase](https://www.tutorchase.com/maths/kuwait-1)). An education official says families must set aside more than **KWD 800** during exam days ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/?p=72684)). Total household spending is often quoted at **KWD 450 million a year** ([Al Borsa News](https://www.alborsaanews.com/?p=1050628)), but no primary study was found. An older KWD 1.3 billion figure ([Al-Anba, 2014](https://www.alanba.com.kw/ar/kuwait-news/506342/19-10-2014-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B5%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%82-%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%A1%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%87%D8%B4-%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%B2%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%AA%D9%82%D9%88%D8%B6-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AC%D8%AA%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%8A/)) applies a per-family average to every family and is best read as an upper bound.

Teachers have needed Ministry permission to tutor since the 1960s, and a 2014 circular banned it outright ([Bray & Hajar, 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20965311221113350)). Enforcement falls mostly on expatriate teachers, who risk contract termination ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-to-sack-expat-teachers-giving-private-tuition-1.1966949)). In 2025 the Ministry began terminating **7,019** non-Kuwaiti teachers ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/7019-teachers-face-termination-as-kuwait-moves-to-cut-kd-42-million-in-costs/)). That shrinks tutor supply and plausibly raises demand for self-study tools. An app is also legally cleaner than a tutor marketplace.

The competition is thin, but the content is free. File sites such as almanahj.com/kw, kwedufiles.com and school-kw.com host official answer keys, teacher booklets and Telegram links for every grade ([almanahj](https://www.almanahj.com/kw/14math); [kwedufiles](https://kwedufiles.com/14math1)). Pythagoras sells Grade 12 Science math video explanations, marketed as a replacement for private lessons ([pythagorasq8.com](https://pythagorasq8.com/)), and KuwaitMath is free ([kuwaitmath.com](https://kuwaitmath.com/)). **Baims** (Kuwait, founded 2017) has 4.4 stars from 2.4k ratings and ranks #90 in Education; it sells courses for USD 18.99–74.99 in-app, and its only AI feature summarises lecture videos ([App Store KW](https://apps.apple.com/kw/app/id1274888149)). Boubyan Bank gave its premium customers 30% off Baims courses ([Boubyan](https://boubyan.bankboubyan.com/en/landing/prime-baims)), a hint that banks are a possible channel. Abwaab does not list Kuwait at all ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/abwaab/id1623367993)). Nobody offers an interactive, step-by-step math tutor in Kuwaiti dialect. The real competitor is a free PDF of the answer key plus a KWD 20 tutor.

### A textbook rewrite and a history of leaks are the two Kuwait risks

The Ministry is rewriting secondary curricula, with math among the priority subjects. New Grade 10 books arrive in 2026/27 and Grade 11 in 2027/28. For Grade 12, the Ministry only says work is ongoing, with no date ([Al-Jarida, 21 May 2026](https://www.aljarida.com/article/132424); [Al-Jarida, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.aljarida.com/article/138450)). Our inference is that a new Grade 12 book arrives in 2028/29 at the earliest. That gives Grade 12 content built in 2027 about two to three exam years of life. Grade 10 content built on the old books is already out of date.

A separate optional "tracks" pilot starts in 12 schools in 2026/27, with 8 tracks including Mathematics and Technology & AI ([Al-Masry in Gulf](https://www.almasryingulf.com/%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AA-2026-2027-8-%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%B5%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%8854-%D9%88%D8%AD/)).

Leaks are a criminal issue in Kuwait. In January 2023 a network selling Grade 11 and 12 exams was exposed: 17 people were detained, 11 of them teachers, one WhatsApp group had about 5,000 members paying KWD 50 a link, and about 20,000 students were treated as suspects ([Al-Jarida, Jan 2023](https://www.aljarida.com/article/11881)). In January 2026 police arrested three people selling what they claimed were exam questions online ([Al-Jarida, 9 Jan 2026](https://www.aljarida.com/article/119800)). Any marketing that sounds like "expected questions" is dangerous there.

The region is also unstable. The minister blamed Iranian aggression for moving schools to distance learning in semester 2 of 2025/26 ([Al-Jarida, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.aljarida.com/article/136311)).

## Saudi Arabia: 12 times bigger, but the math that counts is multiple choice

### Five tracks and a US-style textbook series

Saudi schooling is 6-3-3, with secondary covering Grades 10–12 ([TIMSS 2023 Saudi Arabia](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Saudi-Arabia.pdf)). Under the Tracks system (since 2021/22), Grade 10 is common. Grades 11–12 split into General, Computer Science & Engineering, Health & Life, Business Administration and Sharia ([MoE study plans](https://www.moe.gov.sa/ar/education/generaleducation/StudyPlans/Documents/Study-plans-1445.pdf); [X explainer, 2022](https://x.com/3madAlshareef/status/1549362239693545472)). The three science-type tracks share a strong math sequence. Business gets Statistics, and Sharia lists no math, though these come from unofficial guides ([testmeksa](https://testmeksa.com/articles/saudi-high-school-pathways-guide)).

The books are Arabic adaptations of McGraw-Hill, produced with Obeikan ([TIMSS 2023](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Saudi-Arabia.pdf); [ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1089809.pdf)). Public schools went back to **two semesters** from 2025/26 ([MoE, 5 Aug 2025](https://www.moe.gov.sa/ar/mediacenter/MOEnews/Pages/news1_05082025.aspx)). 2026/27 runs from 23 August 2026 to about 24 June 2027 ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/business/education/saudi-school-calendar-2026-27)).

Grade 12 is split across two books ([almadrasahhub](https://almadrasahhub.com/books/ktab-alryadyat-3-1); [mnhaji](https://www.mnhaji.com/%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%AB%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AB-%D8%AB%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%8A-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%812/)):

| Book | Topics |
|---|---|
| Math 3-1 | Analysing functions (continuity, limits, extrema, average rate of change), exponentials and logs, trig identities, conics |
| Math 3-2 | Vectors in 2D and 3D; polar coordinates and complex numbers with De Moivre; probability and statistics (conditional probability, binomial and normal distributions); a last chapter on limits, derivatives, area under a curve and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus |

That is broader but shallower than the Tunisian bac: calculus arrives only in the final chapter.

### Qudurat and Tahsili replace a national exit exam

No national end-of-school exam was found. University entry runs on school GPA plus two national tests from ETEC's Qiyas centre:

| | Qudurat (General Aptitude Test) | Tahsili (Achievement Test) |
|---|---|---|
| Size | **120 MCQs** in five sections, 2 h 05 | About **110 MCQs**, 2 h 05 |
| Language and mode | Arabic or English; paper or digital | Paper |
| Math content | Science version about **45% quantitative** (arithmetic 23%, geometry 10%, algebra 4%, data 8%): speed math, not curriculum | Grades 10–12 math, alongside physics, chemistry and biology. Math share disputed: **30 of 110 (27%)** in 2025 vs 20 of 95 (about 21%) in 2023 |
| Cost and attempts | SAR 100–200; up to 5 attempts; scores valid 5 years | SAR 100–150 |
| 2026 dates | Digital all year; paper windows | Scientific paper: 13–17 May and 5–9 June |

Sources: [Mualimi guide](https://www.mualimi.app/en/blog/qudurat-gat-exam-complete-guide); [Ajel](https://ajel.sa/thehour/5pij0lduze); [Dirasa Abroad](https://www.dirasaabroad.com/achievement-test/); [PapaMarks](https://papamarks.com/blog/qudurat-tahsili-british-curriculum-saudi); [X post](https://x.com/i/status/1909226432208785438); [Mujtahid](https://mujtahidacademy.com/blog/articles/tahsili-preparation-guide.html); [LiveTutor](https://livetutor.io/ar/curriculum/qudurat-tahsili).

Universities often weight **GPA 30%, Qudurat 30%, Tahsili 40%**, but it varies. King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals publishes 50/40/10 ([PapaMarks](https://papamarks.com/blog/qudurat-tahsili-british-curriculum-saudi)). Humanities programmes often use GPA 50 + Qudurat 50 with no Tahsili ([Cubbbix](https://cubbbix.com/blog/saudi-weighted-score-guide)).

The latest test-taker counts are old. Tahsili had **334,515** takers in 2020 (147,869 male, 186,646 female) ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2032362)). Qudurat had 219,737 candidates in one 2021 paper window, and about 1.06 million applicants in total since the start of 2021 ([SPA](https://www.spa.gov.sa/2295969)). ETEC does not publish past papers. The prep market runs on "tajmee'at", collections of questions students remembered after the test. ETEC has warned that leaking or photographing test questions is a legal violation ([Qiyas on X](https://x.com/EtecQiyas/status/998201721355726848?lang=en)). A Saudi product would therefore need original, timed MCQs that follow ETEC's blueprint, covering three years of content, with step-by-step understanding behind each option. That is a new content model, not a re-skin of the Tunisian one.

### Half a million graduates, and most of them struggle with math

Ministry data from October 2024 count **6.72 million students** in general education, of whom **1,457,650 are in secondary** (745,710 boys, 711,940 girls), taught by 120,810 secondary teachers, across 36,800 schools in total ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1762550)). The 2026 graduating class was **493,862** ([Al-Yaum](https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6687068/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%85/%D9%86%D8%B5%D9%81-%D9%85%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%AE%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AC-%D8%AB%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6-%D9%88%D9%85%D9%83%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%AD%D9%88%D8%B0-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-614)). Of these, 84.5% were Saudi, 61.4% lived in Riyadh, Makkah or the Eastern Province, and about 8 in 10 attended government schools.

The private-school share is reported at anywhere from 17–18% ([Oxford Business Group](https://oxfordbusinessgroup.com/reports/saudi-arabia/2025-report/education-training/prime-expansion-the-establishment-of-new-educational-facilities-aim-to-raise-the-competitiveness-of-universities-and-offer-more-diverse-learning-overview/)) to 28% ([Invest Riyadh](https://investriyadh.ai/dashboards/education-tracker/)).

Math results are poor. In TIMSS 2023, Grade 4 scored 420 (+22 since 2019) and Grade 8 scored 397 ([vision2030.ai](https://vision2030.ai/analysis/saudi-education-outcomes-rankings-vs-schools/); [TIMSS G8 exhibit](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/1-2-1_ach-g8m-dist.pdf)). In PISA 2022, Saudi Arabia scored 389 in math, with only **30% of 15-year-olds at Level 2 or above**, against 69% across the OECD ([OECD country note](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-i-and-ii-country-notes_ed6fbcc5-en/saudi-arabia_f69c2e4b-en.html)). PISA 2025 was essentially flat, at 32% versus 65% for the OECD ([OECD PISA 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2025-results-volume-i-country-notes_2d4ff9ea-en/saudi-arabia_5948545b-en.html)). Roughly **7 in 10** Saudi teenagers are below basic math level. The struggling student is the normal case there.

### Notation matches Kuwait; explanations would need Saudi Arabic

A 118-page Saudi teachers' worksheet pack for Grade 12 (trig, conics, vectors, plus Tahsili practice items) was analysed for the research ([mnhaji worksheet PDF](https://mnhaji.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/worksheet-alriadiaat3-2.pdf)). It uses Latin function names and Greek letters (`tan θ = sin θ / cos θ`), Latin sub-labels such as "(a", and short Latin variables inline in Arabic word problems, with **zero Arabic-Indic digits in about 317,000 characters**. Older Arabised notation (س، ص) is remembered as legacy ([Jehat](https://jehat.net/?act=artc&id=100869)). Math terms are Arabic. An English-medium "Saudi stream" exists in some private schools ([Nada School syllabus](https://nadaschool.edu.sa/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Grade-11-Saudi-Stream_compressed.pdf)), and Qudurat has an English version.

The renderer work needed is the same as for Kuwait. The persona would need Saudi (Najdi or Hijazi) Arabic or light standard Arabic, which is a separate localisation from Kuwaiti.

### Students buy cheap seasonal prep packs from funded rivals

Private math tutoring in Riyadh runs **SAR 90–130 per online session** for Grades 10–11 and **SAR 110–150** for Grade 12 intensive, with home visits up to SAR 220 ([my-teacherprivate](https://my-teacherprivate.com/mathematics-private-teacher/)). Marketplace tutors start at SAR 75 for 50 minutes ([learn.sa](https://learn.sa/tutors/math)).

Market-size figures disagree wildly. One market-research estimate puts digital tutoring at SAR 9 billion in 2024 ([Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1170028)); IMARC puts online tutoring at USD 101.9 million in 2025 ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-online-tutoring-market)); an official 2018 survey found families spending about **SAR 752 million a year** on at-home private lessons ([Al Bawaba](https://www.albawaba.net/business/saudi-parents-spend-sr22-billion-on-education-every-year-report-1082286)). Broad "edtech" estimates of USD 0.8–2.6 billion mostly measure school systems and corporate learning, not this market ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-edtech-market); [GlobalData](https://www.globaldata.com/store/report/saudi-arabia-edtech-market-analysis/)). Exam prep is sold as one-off seasonal packs, not subscriptions:

In July 2026 it bought Almakhfi, a Qudurat/Tahsili platform with 250,000+ students ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2026/07/noon-academy-acquires-almakhfi-strengthen-saudi-edtech-offering-arabic)). Its Saudi listing has 4.5 stars from about 18,000 ratings and mentions no AI. Reviews complain about price and overly long recorded lessons ([App Store SA](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/id1214874641?l=ar-eg)). **Abwaab** has raised USD 27.5 million and sells Saudi Grade 11 and Tahsili products ([Arab News](https://arabnews.pk/node/2177681); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/abwaab/id1623367993)). Smaller players such as AlGooru, GAGA and AILA each raised USD 2.5–4 million in 2024–2026 ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/index.php/2026/03/saudi-edtech-gaga-secures-2-5-million-pre-series-round); [SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/deals/aila-raises-3-million-pre-series-a-to-expand-personalised-learning-platform-14be161b-77c7-4543-a023-0989fc6a064c)). The state gives a lot away: Madrasati shows **11.9 million beneficiaries** ([Madrasati](https://madrasatibeta.moe.gov.sa/)), and textbook barcodes link every lesson to free videos ([TIMSS 2023](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Saudi-Arabia.pdf)).

The biggest strategic threat is **KAUST, which is building an AI math tutor with the Ministry and SDAIA**, one that guides students to the answer by asking questions instead of giving it ([KAUST](https://www.kaust.edu.sa/news/return-of-socrates-kaust-developing-ai-education-for-personalized-learning)). That is TTeClair's idea. If it ships free inside Madrasati, it undercuts any paid app.

### Two Saudi laws hit curriculum apps directly

The **2004 Law of the Production and Marketing of Instructional Materials** covers curriculum content in any format, including material produced abroad ([official translation](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/08/Law-of-the-Production-and-Marketing-of-Instructional-Materials.pdf)). It bans "providing direct answers to textbook questions or exercises", requires a licence before marketing (60 days, approval if the Ministry stays silent, and an exam fee of SAR 200 an hour with a SAR 1,000 minimum), and fines breaches up to **SAR 200,000**. Nobody knows whether the Ministry applies it to apps. A Saudi lawyer has to answer that. TTeClair's hint ladder fits the no-direct-answers rule, but worked solutions to official textbook exercises should stay out of Saudi content.

The **NELC e-learning licence** applies only to providers that issue completion certificates ([NELC rules](https://nelc.gov.sa/en/regulations-and-standards/elearning-executive-rules)). So: no certificates.

A **new General Education Law** takes effect on 20 January 2027. Its rules for online learning are still to be written ([Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/news/opening-a-school-in-saudi-arabia-what-the-new-education-law-means-for-international-educators/)).

Two marketing rules matter as well. Content must not offend Sharia or public policy, so probability examples should use urns, balls and dice rather than cards or betting. Paid influencers must hold a Mawthooq licence ([Arab News](https://arab.news/2xun3)).

## Oman: a Cambridge calculus exam written right to left

### About half of each cohort chooses Advanced math

Oman runs Grades 1–10 as basic education and Grades 11–12 as post-basic. Boys and girls attend separate schools from Grade 5. The year has 207 days and two semesters, with an exam at the end of each, weighted equally ([TIMSS 2023 Oman](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Oman.pdf)).

Since 2022/23, every Grade 11 student chooses **Basic math** (which absorbed the old Applied) or **Advanced math** (which absorbed Pure), and keeps that choice in Grade 12 ([Oman Daily, 2022](https://www.omandaily.om/ampArticle/1017892)). All math from Grade 1 to Grade 12 now uses a Cambridge University Press series adapted for Oman. It reached Grade 12 in 2023/24. The Advanced book states that it is adapted from Cambridge AS & A Level Pure Mathematics 1, Probability & Statistics 1 and Further Mathematics ([Grade 11 Advanced book](https://archive.org/download/11-2_20230408/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%AF%D8%A9%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A9%20%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A9%20%20%D8%B9%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%86%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%A9%2011%20%D9%812%20%20%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8.pdf)).

The Grade 12 Advanced semester-1 exam spreads its 70 marks as follows: circular measure 13, trigonometry 22, limits and continuity 16, differentiation 19, including derivatives of exponentials ([Al-Hinai thesis, 2025](https://www.asu.edu.om/img/Dissertations/Dissertations_2025_m4d6_125438.pdf)). Grade 11 Advanced includes discrete random variables and probability tables, and Grade 12 Basic has a unit on discrete random variables ([teacher plan](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Se0TUwBmxkioowfIrOSjOgIpqYivBADy/export?format=pdf)).

**Advanced math candidates rose from 24,619 (2023/24) to 28,659 (2024/25)**, up 16%. That is about **52%** of the roughly 55,000 GED candidates. Muscat alone had 6,499 ([Al-Hinai thesis](https://www.asu.edu.om/img/Dissertations/Dissertations_2025_m4d6_125438.pdf)). The 2026/27 start-of-year release announced no math changes for Grades 11–12 ([Oman Daily, Sep 2026](https://www.omandaily.om/ampArticle/1209942)).

### The GED is a short-item paper sat twice a year

The General Education Diploma (GED) is set and marked centrally. Grade 12 counts **70% exam, 30% coursework** ([TIMSS 2023 Oman](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Oman.pdf)).

The Advanced math paper lasts **3 hours** and carries **70 marks** across 27 or 29 compulsory items: **12 one-mark MCQs**, short answers worth 2–3 marks, and multi-step items worth 5 marks. It follows Cambridge-style objectives, half knowledge and half application and reasoning, at 40/40/20 low, medium and high difficulty ([Al-Hinai thesis](https://www.asu.edu.om/img/Dissertations/Dissertations_2025_m4d6_125438.pdf)). In January 2025, **61,257** candidates sat in 366 centres, starting with Advanced and Basic math ([Oman Daily, Jan 2025](https://www.omandaily.om/ampArticle/1171718)).

Pass rates for mainstream GED candidates:

| Session | Pass rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2024/25, semester 1 | 80.76% | 54,943 candidates ([Oman Daily](https://www.omandaily.om/ampArticle/1174475)) |
| 2024/25, final | 82.83% | 54,856 candidates ([Oman Observer](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1173565/oman/education/education-ministry-announces-results-of-general-education-diploma-exams)) |
| 2025/26, semester 1 | 81.15% | ([Oman Observer](https://www.omanobserver.om/ampArticle/1184247)) |
| 2025/26, final | **83.37%** | Bilingual private schools: 92.39% ([Oman Observer, Jul 2026](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1192903/oman/education/ged-results-to-be-out-from-today)) |

Students blamed lower semester-1 marks on hard math and physics papers ([Oman Daily](https://www.omandaily.om/ampArticle/1196773)). No official archive of past papers was found, only teacher material on community sites.

The short-item format suits TTeClair's small steps well. But the authoring model of "one exercise per real past paper" would have to rely on unofficial copies.

### Admission runs on a 60/40 average with 98–99% medicine cut-offs

Oman's central admissions office, HEAC, ranks applicants by **60% the average of the programme's required subjects plus 40% the average of all exam subjects** ([HEAC](https://heac.gov.om/index.php/en/about-us)).

In the 2026/27 first round, **46,934** eligible applicants received **33,031 offers** for 34,113 seats ([Oman Observer, Aug 2026](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1194753/oman/education/heac-extends-over-33000-academic-offers-in-first-admission-round)). Cut-offs were steep: **98.15% for men and 99.2% for women** for Sultan Qaboos University General Medicine, and 97.7% for external STEM scholarships. Older guides required Pure Mathematics for engineering and some sciences ([HEAC guide copy](https://www.indemb-oman.gov.in/docs/1629880293StudentGuideofOmaniProgramEn.pdf)). Advanced math is very likely the required subject today, but this was not confirmed.

Government schools hold **876,117 students in 1,298 schools** (2026/27) ([Oman Daily](https://www.omandaily.om/ampArticle/1209942)). TIMSS 2023 scores were **411 in Grade 8** and **421 in Grade 4**, down from 431 in 2019 ([TIMSS G8](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/1-2-1_ach-g8m-dist.pdf); [TIMSS G4](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/1-1-1_ach-g4m-dist.pdf)). That puts Oman above Kuwait and Saudi Arabia but well below the international average.

### Omani textbooks write math in Arabic letters and digits

This is the deal-breaker for now. The official Grade 11 Advanced textbook ([archive.org copy](https://archive.org/download/11-2_20230408/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%AF%D8%A9%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A9%20%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A9%20%20%D8%B9%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%86%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%A9%2011%20%D9%812%20%20%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8.pdf)) and Grade 12 material ([teacher lesson plan](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1ChVDrzZ9hZyEOWOseK_Paic77HGaHa9v/export?format=pdf)) write math right to left. Variables are Arabic letters: س for x, ص for y, د(س) for f(x), هـ for e, ل(س) for P(X). Digits are Arabic-Indic (٢، ٣، ٤/٨١) with a decimal comma. Inequalities and radicals are mirrored, as in ل(٢ > س > ٥), and derivatives are written ءص/ءس, so d/dx(eˣ) = eˣ appears as «ءَ/ءس (هـ^س) = هـ^س».

The current left-to-right renderer (the native LaTeX subset with KaTeX fallback, DECISIONS D1) cannot draw this. Showing Omani students x and f(x) instead would contradict their textbook and exam, about **97% of GED candidates**. Only bilingual private schools teach math in English, with **1,577 GED candidates** ([Oman Daily](https://www.omandaily.om/ampArticle/1174475); [Oman School Finder](https://omanschoolfinder.com/schools/curriculum/bilingual-omani)). Student input would also have to accept Arabic-Indic digits and Arabic letters.

Two points remain open: an actual GED paper was not seen, and the limit and trig notation in the Grade 12 book was not confirmed.

### Tutoring is banned but normal, and the Ministry wants schools on Noor

About **40% of Omani Grade 8 students** take tutoring ([Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/did-you-know-kuwait-has-the-highest-rate-of-private-tutoring-in-the-gulf)). Parents described pre-exam tutoring as something they can no longer avoid, at **more than OMR 20 an hour** and about **OMR 1,200 a year for one subject** ([Oman Daily, 2019](https://www.omandaily.om/ampArticle/29644)). In 2026 they compared it to one more monthly household bill ([Oman Observer, May 2026](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1189900/oman/private-tuition-boon-or-bane)). IGCSE tutors charge OMR 20–40 an hour ([TutorChase](https://www.tutorchase.com/igcse/oman-1)).

Unauthorised tutoring is illegal ([Oman Observer, 2018](https://www.omanobserver.om/ampArticle/49198)).

The Ministry's Noor platform, built with Classera, reaches all government schools in 2025/26, and schools are barred from using unsupervised private platforms ([Oman Observer, Oct 2025](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1178733/oman/omans-schools-go-digital)). Local players include Dorous Oman, a video-lesson site founded in 2015 ([Oman Daily](https://www.omandaily.om/ampArticle/29644)), and Oman Academy. Abwaab does not list Oman.

Omani law is strict on textbooks. Article 77 of the 2023 School Education Law forbids copying or storing Ministry books electronically without written permission, and there is no formal way to get it ([decree.om](https://blog.decree.om/?p=829)).

Saudi median mobile download speed is 194 Mbps ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-saudi-arabia)), so being internet-only is not a problem in Saudi Arabia or Kuwait.

iOS-first works for a Kuwait pilot. It still misses about 60% of Kuwaiti students and about 80% of Omani ones.

### Apple Pay sits on top of every local payment rail

All three markets are mostly cashless. In Saudi Arabia e-payments were **85%** of retail payments in 2025 ([SAMA](https://sama.gov.sa/en-US/MediaCenter/News/Pages/news-1139.aspx)), and mada e-commerce reached SAR 325.2 billion (+65%) ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2639739/business-economy)). In Kuwait non-cash payments reached **85%** of transaction value in Q2 2026, and Apple Pay has been live since 2022 ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/50754/kuwait/other-news/kuwait-expands-digital-payments-non-cash-transactions-reach-85/)); a local KNET gateway costs **2.5% + KWD 0.500** per payment ([Hesabe](https://hesabe-web.pages.dev/products/payment-gateway)). In Oman Apple Pay was **93%** of tokenised card transactions in 2024, its first year ([Oman Observer](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1180349/business/banking/apple-pay-samsung-pay-mobile-transfers-how-customers-make-payments-in-oman)). Noon, Baims and Abwaab all sell through in-app purchase ([Noon](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/id1214874641?l=ar-eg); [Baims](https://apps.apple.com/kw/app/id1274888149)). Parents, often mothers, are the ones paying ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.net/lifestyle/2018/12/23/%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%83%d9%88%d9%8a%d8%aa-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%af%d8%b1%d9%88%d8%b3-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%ae%d8%b5%d9%88%d8%b5%d9%8a%d8%a9-%d9%85%d8%b9%d9%84%d9%85%d9%88%d9%86)). Two consequences: in-app purchase is the obvious first payment path, and the app needs something a parent can see.

### App Store sales need no local company

Apple handles VAT on store sales. It has collected and remitted Saudi VAT on App Store sales since 2018 ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2018/01/25/apple-tax-pricing-changes-foreign-app-stores)), at 15% today ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2020/09/01/apple-raising-app-store-prices-tax-changes)), and it adjusts developer proceeds for Oman's 5% VAT ([App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/app-price-and-tax-changes-from-apple/)). Kuwait has no VAT and reportedly none before 2028 ([TaxInMe](https://www.taxinme.com/articles/kuwait-vat-status-2026.php)). Selling directly on the web would make the Tunisian company a foreign supplier of digital services, liable for VAT registration from the first sale. Oman also requires a local tax representative ([Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/oman)).

A Saudi company is only needed for school or government contracts, local gateways or local licences. Investment registration takes 3–10 business days, plus about a week for the commercial registration ([Alliant](https://alliant.sa/guides/misa-investment-registration-saudi-arabia)).

Saudi rules are about to tighten. Draft amendments published on 6 October 2026 would require a written risk assessment (a DPIA) for any processing of minors' data and for emerging technologies, require registration for anyone processing data of people without legal capacity or sending data abroad, and add ambiguous wording that might demand a copy of the data stored inside Saudi Arabia ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102o5at/sdaia-consults-on-extensive-amendments-to-the-saudi-pdpl-implementing-regulation/)).

For TTeClair this means accounts with a verified guardian, a parent view, and separate consent to send data to an overseas AI provider. Today the app has no accounts and only a pseudonymous local id (docs/PILOT_CONSENT.md), which is good for privacy but not enough for consent. "No personal details in prompts" helps. But skill and event data tied to an account still counts as personal data in all three countries, and the student's own typed questions go to the AI provider.

### No AI law blocks the product, and its design already fits the guidelines

None of the three countries has a binding AI law. Saudi Arabia has non-binding SDAIA ethics principles and generative-AI guidelines ([Nemko](https://digital.nemko.com/regulations/ai-regulation-in-saudi-arabia)). Kuwait has a draft 2025–2028 AI strategy and AI-adoption guidelines that were open for comment until April 2026 ([AI Policy Tracker](https://aipolicytracker.org/jurisdictions/kuwait)). Oman has a policy on safe and ethical AI ([Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/law/2024/12/falqs-ai-regulations-in-the-gulf-cooperation-council-member-states-part-two/)).

Two pieces of evidence back TTeClair's design. First, hints beat answers: in a field experiment with about 1,000 high-school math students, those given an unguarded GPT-4 tutor scored **17% lower** once it was taken away, while a hint-only tutor largely removed that harm ([Penn CHIBE](https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/generative-ai-without-guardrails-can-harm-learning-evidence-from-high-school-mathematics)). Second, language models are weaker at Arabic math: researchers at Nile University found they still need improvement before they handle Arabic math problems well ([Nile University](https://www.nu.edu.eg/publications/mathematical-problem-solving-arabic-assessing-large-language-models)). The hint ladder and the rule that the engine, not the AI, decides correctness are therefore selling points in the Gulf, not just engineering choices.

## The money is real but small at first (our own estimates)

Everything in this section is **our own estimate**, not a sourced figure. The price is USD 15 a month (about KWD 4.6, SAR 56 or OMR 5.8 at the pegs used in the research: SAR 3.75, KWD 1 ≈ USD 3.25, OMR 1 ≈ USD 2.60). We assume a 15% store fee; at 30%, net falls by USD 2.25 per paid month. AI cost is the project's target of USD 3 per paying student per month. VAT is taken out for Saudi Arabia (15%) and Oman (5%). Students pay six months a year in Kuwait and Oman, which have two exam seasons, and four in Saudi Arabia, for a February–May Tahsili season that also matches the local habit of buying seasonal packs.

The target cohorts are 25,000 Grade 12 Science students in Kuwait (the middle of 41,599 × 60% = 24,959 and the 20,000–29,000 range above), 300,000 scientific Tahsili takers in Saudi Arabia (below the 334,515 total Tahsili takers of 2020, and about 61% of the 493,862 graduates), and the 28,659 Advanced math candidates in Oman. We use iPhone shares of 38% for Kuwait, 30% for Saudi Arabia (below the October reading because of the swings) and 19.4% for Oman. Paid shares are set lower in Saudi Arabia because of funded rivals, free government content and the rebuild the product would need there.

Wallet size is not the limit. The Saudi all-phone mid case equals **0.26%** of IMARC's USD 101.9 million online-tutoring estimate, and the Kuwait all-phone mid case (about KWD 34,600) is under **0.01%** of the reported KWD 450 million tutoring spend. What limits revenue is reach, conversion and how many phones the app runs on.

Saudi Arabia is only about **twice Kuwait in the iOS mid case** (USD 81,000 vs 42,750 gross), despite being 12 times bigger, because lower conversion and a shorter season eat most of the size advantage. Its real upside, not counted here, is Android, Qudurat (taken by almost every applicant) and Grades 10–11. Oman, meanwhile, becomes **bigger than Kuwait once Android and Arabic-script math exist** (USD 128,970 vs 112,500 all-phone gross), which is the argument for building those two pieces eventually, not now.

Free users will eat the margin. None of these figures include AI cost for free users, teacher-review fees, legal opinions or marketing. If free users trigger AI calls, they consume the USD 8–10 a month each payer leaves, so the USD 3 AI figure should also be re-measured with Kuwaiti Arabic output.

**Sell through in-app purchase only, in two forms.** Test a monthly plan (about KWD 4.5) against a semester pass (around KWD 20, untested). Keep the free tier on prepared replies only, so it never calls the AI.

### Leave these alone for now

**Oman.** Do not start an Oman launch or the right-to-left Arabic math renderer. It is a large project (Arabic letters, Arabic-Indic digits, mirrored signs, Arabic-digit input) for a market that is 81% Android. The bilingual English niche, at 1,577 candidates, is too small to justify a release on its own.

**Saudi Tahsili and Qudurat.** Do not build these until Kuwait proves that students pay. When you do, never use tajmee'at or recalled questions, issue no certificates, publish no worked answers to official textbook exercises, and keep cards and betting out of probability examples. Get the Saudi legal pack done first: SDAIA registration, a data protection officer, standard contract clauses with the cloud and AI providers, a risk assessment for minors, and a lawyer's view on the 2004 instructional-materials law.

**Saudi and Omani government deals.** In Saudi Arabia, KAUST, SDAIA and the big platforms hold that ground. In Oman, schools must use Noor.

**Local companies and web checkout.** Both trigger VAT registration and local entities before there is revenue to justify them.

**Kuwaiti Grades 10–11 content.** Those books are being replaced in 2026/27 and 2027/28.

**Marketing.** Avoid anything that hints at "expected questions" in Kuwait. Do not use serving public-school teachers as tutors or faces of the app in any of the three countries.

## Conclusion

Expanding to the Gulf is really three separate engineering decisions, not one market decision. Kuwait needs short math to sit inline in Arabic sentences, which the project has already half-solved on iOS. Saudi Arabia needs a new kind of content: original, timed MCQs with understanding behind each option, for admission tests rather than school exams. Oman needs right-to-left Arabic-script math, which no current part of the stack can produce. Taking them in that order means each market pays for the next step instead of all three being built on hope. It also turns the project's strictest rules into assets: engine-checked correctness answers the weakness of AI in Arabic math, the hint ladder matches both the learning evidence and a Saudi ban on direct textbook answers, and refusing to copy official corrections keeps the content legally clean.

The research also changes who the product is for. In Kuwait, nine in ten Science students now pass, so TTeClair must sell as a way to raise marks for students chasing Medicine and Engineering as much as a rescue for strugglers. In Saudi Arabia, the struggling student is the majority, but a free, state-backed Socratic tutor may arrive first. The cheapest useful thing to learn in the next two months is whether 10–20 Kuwaiti students, shown two exercises in their own dialect, would ask their parents to pay.
